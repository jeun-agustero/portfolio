How I build creative

The same loop on every account. The order matters: most weak ads I audit skipped straight to step four.

  1. Research

    Reviews, comments, competitors and the Ad Library. What buyers want, in their words.

  2. Diagnose

    Market desire, sophistication and awareness. Where the buyer is decides what the ad has to do.

  3. Concept

    Persona, desire and offer. One clear bet per concept, so a result teaches something.

  4. Angle and hook

    Several ways in, each with its own hook, all feeding one body.

  5. Build

    I write the copy and brief or build the asset: statics, UGC-style and AI video, carousels.

  6. Read and iterate

    Hook rate, hold rate, conversion rate, cost per result. Then iterate the winner.

Webinar funnel, wellnessLead genMay to Sep 2026

Concepts beat hooks, measured on real sign-ups

The bet

A new concept changes who stops scrolling. A new hook on the same concept mostly changes who clicks.

Built

Ran concept-led ad sets against hook-led ones, and moved the tracking to GTM and Stape, because Ads Manager was reporting 891 sign-ups where the funnel's own sheet had 646.

The read

On real sign-ups, concept-led ad sets cost 28% less per sign-up than hook-led ones.

$9.33per real sign-up, concept-led
$12.96per real sign-up, hook-led
$6.24per real sign-up on the best single concept

Sign-ups did not turn into sales: 13 front-end sales in five months. The next test belongs on the offer and the follow-up, not the ads.

Before You Buy a New AC

Hypothesis
Homeowners who think their AC is failing are about to spend big. Framing the free inspection as the smart check before an AC replacement should bring in higher-intent leads and give the $500 off a clear reason to matter.
Persona
A DFW homeowner whose AC runs nonstop and whose bill keeps rising. They have an AC quote or are about to ask for one. They blame the AC.
Angle
Reframe plus cost contrast. The AC is not broken, it is outnumbered. A new AC under the same attic faces the same heat.
Format
Phone-shot demo. A company tech walks from the outdoor unit up into the attic and explains on the way.
HookLineVisual
H1"Before you replace this... check up there."Tech taps the outdoor AC unit, then tilts the camera up at the roof.
H2"Got an AC quote this summer? Watch this first."Tech with a clipboard in a driveway, talking to camera.
H3"Your AC might not be broken. It might be outnumbered."Tech on the attic ladder, half inside the hatch.
Shared body scriptVisual direction
"Here's what most DFW homeowners never get told."Tech climbing the ladder.
"In summer, an attic around here sits around 150 degrees."Inside the attic. On-screen text: "About 150°F up here in summer."
"If the insulation up here has gone flat, or was never enough, that heat pushes down into your rooms all day."Tech presses a hand into flat, thin insulation. Close-up.
"Your AC isn't broken. It's outnumbered. It's fighting this attic all day long."Cut to the AC unit running outside.
"So before you pay for a new AC, let us come look. It's free."Tech back on the ground, talking to camera.
"We take photos. You see what's up here. You get a straight number."Tech showing inspection photos on a phone.
"And if it doesn't need work, we tell you that too."Tech shrugs. Honest tone.

UGC concept and staticCustomer voice

Real Homeowner, Real Attic

Hypothesis
A real DFW homeowner showing their own attic photos will lower lead cost and lift lead quality against company-voice ads, because proof from a neighbor beats a claim from a contractor.
Concept
"Here is what was in my attic." A phone-filmed interview in the customer's home, cut with their real before and after inspection photos.
Rule
Customer lines are not scripted. I write the questions, we keep the best real answers, and we get written consent for name, face, home and photos.
Hooks
  • On-screen: "We went back to a house we fixed [X] weeks ago. We asked one question."
  • Off camera: "What was this room like in August?" Customer answers in the room that used to be hot.
  • On-screen: "This was [name]'s attic. This is it now." Hard cut, before photo to after photo.
Interview prompts
  • What made you call?
  • What did you think when you saw the attic photos?
  • What did you notice that first night?
  • What would you tell a neighbor?
Static: review card
  • Headline options: "See what was in [name]'s attic." / "2,500 DFW homes. Here's one of them." / "Your neighbor checked the attic. Here's what we found."
  • Real before photo left, after photo right, large.
  • Review card in the lower third: customer photo, five stars, one real review line of 12 words or fewer.
  • The actual attic, never stock. Logo small in the corner.
  • Button: "Free Attic Check", with "$500 off this month" under it.

Customer language banks from reviews and comments, competitor and Ad Library teardowns, awareness mapping, concept and angle shortlists with the reasoning attached.
